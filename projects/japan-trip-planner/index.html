Past · how it was madeProblem and design goal

Problem
A group trip keeps its plan in one place and its money in another. People need to know what's next, what they owe and when — on a phone, often one-handed in a station or terminal.
Design goal
Put the plan and the money in one place, and never blur money that is certain with money that is a guess.

Decision
Every cost is marked paid, committed (booked, not fully paid) or estimated, and the three always add up to the projected cost. With no budget set, the app says 'No budget set' rather than inventing a figure.
Why it mattered
Money a group has actually spent and money it might spend lead to different decisions; mixing them hides risk.

Decision
A gate is a field filled in on the day through a deliberately small form, and the app records when it was set. A live-status link sits beside it; flights without a number say so instead of showing a dead button.
Why it mattered
Airports assign gates a few hours before departure, so the app doesn't pretend to know — and a gate from yesterday is worse than no gate at all.

Past · how it was madePlayer experience

Intended experience
Open the app and see the one thing you most likely need: before the trip, the next flight; during it, today's next stop and tonight's hotel.
Design tools used to shape it
  • Mobile-first layout with a bottom bar per section and a single + that adds anything from anywhere.
  • Three money states on every cost — paid, committed, estimated — that always add up to the projected total.
  • 'Still to sort' health check listing only what needs a look (nights with nowhere to stay, payments due, missing prices).
  • 'What changed?' line after an edit, e.g. projected cost before → after.
