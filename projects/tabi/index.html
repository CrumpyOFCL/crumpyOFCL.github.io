Tabi

A group trip planner for any trip and any group. Invite-only, phone-first, and built so a different trip, group size or budget does not turn into clutter. A Japan trip was the first real use.

Live, invite-only. A cover image and the remaining write-up slots are still to come.

The live app is invite-only. This page links to the public sign-in screen and does not show trip data, names or invite codes.

Checkpoint 2What I did

  • Designed and built Tabi: more than one trip on an account, each with its own invite-only group (viewer and editor roles); a day-by-day itinerary with map pins; a budget with a whole-group / per-person toggle and a budget-ceiling warning; live currency conversion; weather; a home screen that shows Today's plan during a trip and a countdown before it; Google sign-in.
  • Designed and built the earlier screens too: sign-in gate, trip overview, budget, plan, stays, flights, food, trains and people.
  • Reworked most of the code and the visual design to get a mobile-first, phone-app feel.
  • Iterated in small pull requests: 60 over eight days, 57 merged.

Checkpoint 3What it is

Features

Trips
More than one trip on an account. Each trip has its own invite-only group, with viewer and editor roles.
Itinerary
A day-by-day plan with map pins.
Budget
A whole-group / per-person toggle, and a warning when spending crosses a budget ceiling. Costs stay marked paid, committed or estimated. With no budget set, the app says "No budget set".
Currency
Live currency conversion.
Weather
Weather for the trip.
Home
Today's plan while a trip is on. A countdown before it starts.
Sign-in
Google sign-in. The app is mobile-first and meant to feel like a phone app.

Checkpoint 5Design decisions

Never show a guess as if it were certain
  1. Assumption (published)
  2. Test (pending)
  3. Evidence (pending)
  4. Insight (pending)
  5. Decision (published)
  6. Iteration (published)
Decision
Every cost is marked paid, committed (booked, not fully paid) or estimated, and the three always add up to the projected cost. With no budget set, the app says 'No budget set' rather than inventing a figure.
Why it mattered
Money a group has actually spent and money it might spend lead to different decisions; mixing them hides risk.
Result
Added in the 'precise money states' iteration (PR #56) and carried onto the plan (PR #59).
Gates, honestly
  1. Assumption (published)

Decision
A gate is a field filled in on the day through a deliberately small form, and the app records when it was set. A live-status link sits beside it; flights without a number say so instead of showing a dead button.
Why it mattered
Airports assign gates a few hours before departure, so the app doesn't pretend to know — and a gate from yesterday is worse than no gate at all.

Decision
Accounts are created only from an invite that a trip owner issues; sign-in is through Google.
Why it mattered
The app holds a private group's plans and payments; nobody should be able to wander in.
Options considered
Password sign-in and Apple Sign In were tried; Apple was removed (PR #21), then sign-in was made Google-only (PR #24).

Checkpoint 6Player experience

Intended experience
Before a trip, the home screen is a countdown. During a trip, it is Today's plan. A Japan trip was the first real use of that.
Design tools used to shape it
  • Mobile-first layout with a bottom bar per section and a single + that adds anything from anywhere.
  • Three money states on every cost — paid, committed, estimated — that always add up to the projected total.
  • 'Still to sort' health check listing only what needs a look (nights with nowhere to stay, payments due, missing prices).
  • 'What changed?' line after an edit, e.g. projected cost before → after.

Checkpoint 7Reflection

The design challenge is one app that stays flexible across different trips, group sizes and budgets without clutter. Tabi keeps each trip in its own invite-only group, breaks the plan into days with map pins, and lets the same budget be read for the whole group or per person, with a warning at the ceiling. The home screen changes job: a countdown before the trip, Today's plan during it. A Japan trip was the first real use. It was the test case, not a limit on who the app is for.

Checkpoint 8Problem and design goal

Problem
One app has to serve any trip and any group. The plan and the money need to live together, and the app has to stay clear when the trip, the group size and the budget all change. A Japan trip was the first real use, often checked on a phone.
Design goal
Make one planner flexible across different trips, group sizes and budgets without clutter, and never blur money that is certain with money that is a guess.
