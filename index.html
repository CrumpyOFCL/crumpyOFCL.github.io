How I work

  1. 1 Research

    Find out what players and the brief actually need before building.

    From my work Evidence pending One real example of research that shaped a design (e.g. reference study, player interviews).

  2. 2 Prototype

    Get the idea into something playable as cheaply as possible.

    From my work LIT_Flux Mechanics Showcase and GDT2 are browser-playable Unity prototypes on my itch.io page.

  3. 3 Test

    Put it in front of players and watch what they do.

    From my work Built an in-build debug console for playtesters on A Course In Time — noclip, ability grants, scene jumps and objective control without an editor.

  4. 4 Analyse

    Turn observations into findings, and findings into a decision.

    From my work Evidence pending One playtest finding and the analysis behind it.

  5. 5 Iterate

    Change the design, then check the change did what it was meant to.

    From my work Evidence pending One before/after change with the reason for it.

Tyler Crump

Game designer · gameplay and tools

I build the parts of games that have to keep working when everything else changes — and I like it when everything changes.

Most of my work sits under the surface: the systems that hold a level together, and the editor tools that let a designer build twenty rooms instead of two. I work in Unity and C#, with FMOD for audio.

About

I like games where the rules are the level. My flagship project, A Course In Time, is a puzzle-platformer about one building across time — so most of what I've built is the machinery underneath: the switch between eras, the generators that make puzzles, the save system that remembers them, and the tools that let other people test them.
