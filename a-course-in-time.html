Concept

One building, two eras, one set of hands.

The hotel is the puzzle. Each era hides part of a route: a floor that has collapsed in the Present may still stand in the Past, and a wall that blocks the Past may be rubble in the Present. Progress comes from abilities, never combat.

Core loop

  1. 01Explore the hotel
  2. 02Find a path neither era allows
  3. 03Switch eras and use abilities
  4. 04Solve the room
  5. 05Unlock an ability that opens paths you could already see

↺ repeat with new tools

Abilities

Time Switch
Shift between Present and Past. Platforms appear, walls vanish, hazards change.
Time Beam
Freeze objects across both timelines; bounce beams off mirrors to hit targets around corners.
Dash
A short burst of speed to cross gaps and avoid hazards.
Double Jump
A second jump in mid-air to reach new areas.
Box Pull

Design decisions

What I built, and why it works that way.

  1. 01Time closing around the player is a death, not a glitch

    Goal
    Switching eras should read as a rule of the world that players can learn and plan around.
    Constraint
    Geometry that exists in only one era can appear exactly where the player is standing at the moment they switch.
    Decision
    Treat that overlap as a death, detected by a crush check, instead of pushing the player out or letting them clip through.
    How it works
    The era system swaps collision layers, object state and physics in the same step. The crush check catches the moment solid geometry from the other era would occupy the player's space.
    For the player
    Standing in the wrong place when you switch always has the same outcome. It becomes something to plan around rather than a bug to report.

  2. 02Saving the world's state, not just the player's position

    Goal
    A reloaded game should remember which doors are open and which puzzles are done.
    Constraint
    The hotel is interconnected, with shortcuts and puzzle-gated doors, so progress lives in the world, not only in where the player is standing.
    Decision
    Build saving around an objective ledger: a record of completed objectives the world reads back on load.
    How it works
    The save system records objectives alongside the skill tree's save/load, so rooms restore to their solved state.
    For the player
    Coming back to the game doesn't mean re-solving rooms or finding shortcuts locked again.

  3. 03A debug console in the playtest build

    Goal
    Playtesters and teammates need to reach and repeat any situation quickly.
    Constraint
    Playtests run on builds, not in the Unity editor, and progression is gated by abilities, so late rooms normally take a full playthrough to reach.
    Decision
    Ship playtest builds with an in-build console instead of relying on editor-only shortcuts.
    How it works
    Noclip, ability grants, scene jumps and objective control, all available without the editor.
    For testers
    Testers can jump straight to the room being tested instead of replaying the game to reach it.

My contribution

Systems and tools I built.

Programming was shared with the team; Payton Saunders was lead programmer. These are the parts I built.

  • The era system: collision layers, object state and physics swapped in step, with the crush check.
  • Moving-platform passenger behaviour, player dash and jump, and player audio.
  • The Time Beam mechanic.
  • A skill tree with save/load, the objective-ledger save system and the HUD.
  • Procedural puzzle generators with Scene-view grid painting. Pipe networks use a connectivity solver. Elemental puzzles choose their elements by counting how many valid solutions each set allows.
  • The in-build debug console for playtesters.
  • WebGL audio compatibility fixes and URP particle shaders.
